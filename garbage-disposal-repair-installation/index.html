Garbage disposal repair for jams, leaks, and dead units

Garbage disposal repair means clearing a jam, resetting a tripped motor, or fixing a leak at one of the unit's connections. When the disposal body itself is leaking or the motor has failed, installation of a new unit is the fix.

Disposals fail in a few predictable ways. A humming unit that will not spin is usually jammed. One that does nothing at all has often tripped its reset button. A leak from the bottom of the housing usually means the internal seals have worn through.

Disposals also sit at the center of the kitchen drain, connected to the sink, the drain line, and often the dishwasher. A problem with any of those connections can look like a disposal problem, so each is checked before anything is replaced.

What to try before you call

These steps fix a surprising number of disposal problems. Cut the power first.

  • 1Turn off the switch and the breaker. Never reach into a disposal that could start.
  • 2Press the red reset button. It is on the bottom of the unit. A tripped overload stops the motor to protect it.
  • 3Free a jam with the hex key. Most units have a hex socket in the center of the bottom. Turning it back and forth frees a stuck plate.
  • 4Remove debris with tongs. Look into the chamber with a flashlight and pull out anything stuck, never with your hand.
  • 5Run cold water and test. Restore power, run cold water, and switch it on. If it still hums or leaks, call.

The dishwasher connection matters

Most dishwashers drain through the disposal. When a new disposal goes in, the knockout plug inside its dishwasher inlet has to be removed, or the dishwasher will not drain. It is one of the most common mistakes in do-it-yourself installs.

The IPC and dishwasher installation guidelines both address how that drain connects. The line also needs protection from backflow, usually an air gap at the sink or a high loop under the counter, so dirty sink water cannot flow back into the dishwasher. Air-gap leaks at the sink top are a common call on their own.

What to keep out of the disposal

Grease is the biggest problem, for the disposal and for the line beyond it. It passes through the unit as liquid, then hardens farther down the pipe. The City of Surprise's grease campaign exists for exactly this reason.

Fibrous and starchy foods cause the most jams. Celery strings, onion skins, corn husks, and potato peels wrap around the plate or form a paste that clogs the drain. Coffee grounds and eggshells add grit that settles in the trap.

Homes on a septic system should use a disposal sparingly, if at all. Ground food adds solids to the tank and can shorten the time between septic tank pumpings.

Every disposal in town drains into the same system, whether the kitchen is near Surprise Stadium or out past Loop 303, and that system serves one of the fastest-growing cities in Maricopa County. Keeping grease and fibrous scraps out protects your line and the city's.

Insurance and disposal leaks

A worn-out disposal is not covered by insurance. Water damage from a sudden connection failure may be, but slow leaks under the sink are usually excluded.
