Sewer line repair that starts with seeing the pipe

Sewer line repair is fixing a damaged section of the lateral, the private pipe that carries waste from your house to the public sewer main. Repairs range from replacing one broken joint to lining a longer stretch from the inside without digging a trench.

In most cases the lateral from the house to the city main belongs to the homeowner, so its upkeep falls to you. That is worth knowing before a backup, not during one.

Every repair here starts with a camera. Guessing from the surface leads to digging in the wrong place, and a short video run shows exactly what failed, where it is, and how deep the pipe sits.

What to do during a sewage backup

A backup is an emergency because the waste has nowhere to go but up. These steps limit the mess until the line is open.

  • 1Stop all water use. Every flush, shower, and load of laundry adds to what is coming back up.
  • 2Keep people and pets away. Sewage carries bacteria. Stay off wet flooring and keep children out of the area.
  • 3Do not use chemical openers. They rarely reach a main-line blockage and make the water dangerous for whoever opens the line.
  • 4Open the cleanout cap only if safe. Loosening the exterior cleanout can let sewage drain outside instead of into the house. Stand back while it releases.
  • 5Photograph the damage. Pictures of the backup and any wet materials help with a sewer backup claim if your policy covers one.

Why laterals fail in the West Valley

Soil movement is one cause. Much of the West Valley has layers of caliche, a hard, cemented soil, and pipes laid through disturbed ground can settle unevenly over the years. A small sag is enough to collect debris and start repeat clogs.

Roots are the other common cause. Trees and shrubs planted in front yards send roots toward any moisture, and a slightly open joint is an easy target. Once inside, the roots grow and catch paper and grease.

Grease makes both problems worse. A pipe that is partly blocked by roots or a sag fills up faster when grease coats the walls, which is part of why the city asks residents to keep fats and oils out of the drains.

Much of Surprise was built on former Maricopa County farmland, and yards near Grand Avenue and the Agua Fria side of town can sit on fill that has settled over the years. That movement is one reason a camera look pays off before anyone digs.

Repair options, from smallest to largest

The smallest repair that actually fixes the problem is the right one.

Excavate and replace one section

When the damage is in one place, a small excavation exposes that section, and it is cut out and replaced with new pipe and couplings.

The camera footage marks the depth and location first, so the hole is only as big as it needs to be.

Best when

Damage is limited to one spot

Often the lowest cost when the spot is under open yard rather than concrete.

Trenchless lining from the inside

A resin-soaked liner is pulled into the old pipe and cured in place, creating a new pipe inside the old one. It seals cracks and joints without a trench.

The existing pipe must be cleared and still hold its shape for lining to work.

Best when

The line runs under a driveway or landscaping

Avoids tearing up hardscape, though the finished pipe is slightly smaller inside.

Replace a longer run

When a long stretch has failed, replacing that run with new pipe may be the lasting fix. Very long failures move into full replacement territory.

This is also when a new cleanout can be added if the old line lacked a good access point.

Best when

Several defects sit close together

What insurance usually says about sewer lines

Standard policies often exclude the lateral itself and treat root damage and wear as maintenance. Sewer backup coverage, if you added it, may pay for cleanup inside the house. Check your policy before assuming either.
