Water heater replacement for hot garages and hard water

Water heater installation is the removal of an old unit and the setup of a new one with correct venting, gas or electrical supply, water connections, and safety valves. Most Surprise homes keep the heater in the garage, which shapes almost every choice in the job.

A tank that leaks from the bottom has failed and cannot be patched. When that happens, same-day replacement is usually the goal, and our emergency line is open 24/7 for it. When the old unit still works but is getting old, a planned replacement lets you compare options without a cold shower forcing the decision.

Either way, the new heater should be sized for your household, matched to your water, and installed to the 2024 International Plumbing Code that Surprise adopted effective January 1, 2026.

Which type of water heater fits your home

Each type has a real trade-off. The best pick depends on your fuel, your space, and your water.

Standard gas tank

A gas tank heats quickly and recovers fast after a long shower. It is often the simplest like-for-like swap when a gas line and vent already exist.

It needs a working flue and proper combustion air, both checked at install.

Good fit

Homes already on gas

Lowest disruption when replacing an existing gas unit.

Standard electric tank

An electric tank has no flue or gas line, so it can go in more locations. It recovers more slowly than gas, which matters for larger households.

Hard water attacks the heating elements, so a softener or regular flushing extends its life.

Good fit

All-electric homes

Simple installation with no venting to maintain.

Heat pump water heater

A heat pump unit pulls heat from the surrounding air to warm the water. It uses much less electricity than a standard electric tank.

A hot Surprise garage is actually an advantage here, because warm air gives the heat pump more to work with for much of the year.

Good fit

Electric homes with garage space

Needs room around the unit for airflow and a drain for condensate.

Tankless water heater

A tankless unit heats water only as it flows, so it never runs out. It also frees up floor space in the garage.

It is the type most sensitive to scale. Without soft water or regular descaling, the heat exchanger clogs and performance drops.

Good fit

Homes that can keep up with descaling

Gas units may need a larger gas line and new venting.

A tank that is too small runs out mid-shower. One that is too big wastes energy keeping water hot.

Safety checklist for a garage water heater install

These are safety requirements, not upgrades. Ask about each one on any install quote.

  • 1Temperature and pressure relief valve. Every tank needs one, with a discharge pipe running to a safe spot where hot water cannot hit anyone.
  • 2Burner raised off the floor. Gas appliances with a flame in a garage generally must sit at least 18 inches above the floor to keep fuel vapors away from the burner.
  • 3Expansion tank where required. If a check valve or pressure reducing valve closes the system, heated water needs somewhere to expand.
  • 4Drain pan and venting. A pan under the tank catches small leaks, and gas units need a properly sloped, sealed flue.
  • 5A permit when the city requires it. Many water heater replacements in Surprise need a permit and inspection. Ask whether yours is included.

Signs it is time to replace, not repair

Age is the first clue. The serial number on the data label usually encodes the build year, and a heater older than about 10 years that begins to leak or rumble is rarely worth fixing. Rusty hot water or a pool under the tank means the lining has failed.

Heavy scale is the second clue. If flushing brings out chunks of mineral and the tank still pops and bangs, sediment has likely damaged the bottom. When a heater only has a failed part, like an element or gas valve, water heater repair is often the better value.

Softening the water before a new tank goes in protects the investment. Our water softener installation page explains how a hardness test sets that up.

Water heater permits here are handled by the City of Surprise rather than Maricopa County, and the water feeding the tank comes from EPCOR or the city depending on your address. Homes from the 2000s boom around Marley Park and north of Loop 303 are now reaching their second or third tank.

When insurance helps with a failed tank

Insurance generally does not buy a new water heater when the old one wears out. If the tank bursts and floods the house, the resulting water damage may be covered, so photograph it before cleanup.
