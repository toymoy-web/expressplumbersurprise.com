Emergency water damage restoration, around the clock

Water damage restoration is the process of removing water, drying the structure, and repairing or replacing damaged materials after a leak or flood. It is separate from fixing the pipe that caused it. The plumbing repair stops the source, and restoration deals with what the water already reached.

Speed matters because water keeps spreading. It wicks up drywall, soaks under flooring, and settles into cabinets. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to help prevent mold.

The restoration line answers 24/7. Calls come after burst supply lines, water heater failures, slab leaks, appliance hoses, and monsoon storms that push water under doors.

What to do before the restoration team arrives

These steps limit damage and protect your claim.

  • 1Stop the source. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or appliance is leaking.
  • 2Cut power to wet areas. Only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • 3Photograph everything. Take pictures and video of the water, the source, and damaged belongings before cleanup.
  • 4Move valuables. Lift electronics, documents, and furniture off wet floors if it is safe.
  • 5Call your insurer. Report the loss early and keep receipts for anything you spend.

How restoration works, step by step

Restoration follows a set sequence based on the IICRC S500 standard for water damage.

StageWhat happensWhy it matters
InspectionMoisture meters and thermal cameras map where water has spread.Hidden water behind walls and under floors is found.
Water removalStanding water is extracted with pumps and vacuums.The faster water leaves, the less soaks in.
Removal of wet materialsSaturated carpet pad, drywall, or insulation is removed where needed.Some materials cannot be dried effectively.
DryingAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry standards.Drying is verified with meters, not guessed.
RepairDrywall, flooring, and cabinets are replaced or repaired.The home is put back together.

Monsoon season and water damage in Surprise

Monsoon storms bring sudden, heavy rain to the West Valley in summer. Water can back up at doors, flow into garages, and enter through roof leaks, often at the same time other homes nearby are calling.

Storm water is treated differently from clean supply-line water. Water that has crossed the ground or picked up contaminants needs more careful handling and cleaning.

If the damage came from a plumbing failure, the leak must be repaired first. That work is covered on our burst pipe repair page, while restoration handles the drying and rebuild.

Monsoon storms cross Maricopa County every summer, and in Surprise runoff can pool in low spots along streets like Grand Avenue. The City of Surprise handles streets and storm drains, while water that gets inside your home is a restoration job.
