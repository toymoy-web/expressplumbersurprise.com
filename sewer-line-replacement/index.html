When a sewer line needs replacing, not repairing

Sewer line replacement is installing a new lateral, or a long stretch of it, in place of a pipe that has failed along much of its length. It is the right answer when repeated repairs would cost more than a new line and still leave old pipe in the ground.

A camera inspection makes the call. One bad joint or a single crack is a spot repair. Several breaks, a long sag, or pipe that has lost its shape points toward replacement.

Replacement is also a chance to fix design problems, like adding an accessible cleanout near the house or correcting the slope of a line that has settled.

Replacing a lateral in Surprise means working with the City of Surprise on the permit and the connection to its main, and calling Arizona 811, which marks buried lines across Maricopa County. Yards near Grand Avenue with mature landscaping often favor trenchless methods.

How a new sewer line goes in

The method depends on what sits above the line and the condition of the old pipe.

Traditional excavation

A trench is dug along the line, the old pipe is removed, and new pipe of an IPC-approved material such as PVC is laid at the right slope and bedded properly.

It works in almost any condition and allows a full inspection of the new line before backfill.

Good fit when

The line runs under open yard

Most straightforward when landscaping is easy to restore.

Trenchless pipe bursting

A bursting head is pulled through the old pipe, breaking it outward while new pipe follows behind it. Only small access pits are needed at each end.

It suits lines under driveways or mature landscaping where a trench would be costly to restore.

Good fit when

Driveways or hardscape above

Needs a clear path and suitable soil conditions.

Lining a long section

Where the old pipe still holds its shape, a cured-in-place liner can create a new pipe inside it along a long run.

It is not an option for crushed or collapsed pipe.

Good fit when

The pipe is cracked but round

Our sewer line repair page covers lining for shorter sections.

What to do before a sewer replacement starts

A little planning keeps the job smooth.

  • 1Get the camera footage. It shows the extent of the damage and supports the choice of method.
  • 2Confirm the permit. Lateral work is usually permitted and inspected by the city.
  • 3Make sure Arizona 811 is called. Buried utilities must be marked before digging begins.
  • 4Clear the work area. Move vehicles, planters, and outdoor furniture from along the line.
  • 5Plan water use. You will not be able to use drains for part of the job. Ask how long.

Insurance and full sewer replacement

Insurance usually does not pay to replace an old or failing sewer lateral. Service line endorsements, if you have one, may cover some costs. Read the policy before work starts.
