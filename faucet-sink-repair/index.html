Faucet and sink repair for everyday leaks

Faucet repair means stopping a drip or restoring flow by replacing worn internal parts such as the cartridge, O-rings, or seats. Sink repair covers the drain side: the tailpiece, P-trap, basket strainer, and the connections under the cabinet.

Federal standards cap new bathroom faucets at 2.2 gallons per minute, and WaterSense-labeled models use 1.5 or less. A drip that seems small adds up. A faucet dripping once a second wastes gallons every day, and a slow leak under a sink can soak the cabinet floor for weeks before anyone notices the soft spot or the smell.

In Surprise, hard water is often behind both. Scale wears cartridges, clogs aerators, and cakes the base of the spout, so faucets here tend to need attention sooner than the same model in a soft-water city.

Faucet cartridges wear fastest where water is hardest, and in this part of Maricopa County that varies by supplier, EPCOR in some neighborhoods and the City of Surprise in others. Kitchens in the older homes near Bell Road tend to show the scale first.

What to do before the plumber arrives

A few minutes of prep makes the repair faster.

  • 1Close the angle stops. The two small valves under the sink stop water to that faucet only.
  • 2Find the brand. Look for a logo on the handle or spout. Cartridges are specific to each maker.
  • 3Empty the cabinet. Clearing the space under the sink saves time and keeps your things dry.
  • 4Put a bowl under the drip. If a drain is leaking, a container keeps water off the cabinet floor.
  • 5Check the aerator. Unscrew the tip of the spout. A cake of white scale there can explain low flow on its own.

Repair the faucet or replace it?

A good faucet can be repaired for years. A worn-out one costs more to keep fixing.

A quality faucet with a drip usually just needs a new cartridge or seals. Parts for major brands are widely available.

Scale and faucets in Surprise

Scale shows up first at the aerator, as white grit that cuts flow. Soaking the aerator in vinegar clears it, but scale is also building inside the cartridge where you cannot see it.

If faucets keep stiffening and dripping every couple of years, your water may be on the hard end. That same scale is shortening the life of your water heater, so a tap test is worth the few minutes it takes.
