A softener sized to your water, not someone else's

A water softener is a tank of resin beads that swaps the calcium and magnesium in your water for sodium or potassium through ion exchange. The result is water that leaves less scale in pipes, water heaters, and fixtures.

In Surprise, the right softener depends on which utility serves your address. EPCOR's Agua Fria district blends Colorado River water from the Central Arizona Project with Agua Fria River water and groundwater from 15 wells. The city's own Mountain Vista system relies only on groundwater, drawn from 16 wells.

Published hardness numbers for Surprise do not agree, and some differ by more than three times. That is why every install starts with a test at your tap. The reading sets the softener size and its regeneration setting.

The West Valley of Maricopa County leans heavily on groundwater, which is part of why readings from older neighborhoods near Surprise Stadium can differ from newer builds north of Loop 303. A tap test settles it for your own address.

What your hardness reading means

Your test result falls into one of these bands. The band tells you whether a softener is worth it.

ReadingWater Quality Association bandWhat it means at home
0 to 3.5 gpgSoftA softener is rarely needed. Filtration may still help with taste.
3.5 to 7 gpgModerately hardSome spotting on glass and slow scale in the water heater. A softener is optional.
7 to 10.5 gpgHardVisible scale on aerators and showerheads. A softener protects heaters and fixtures.
Over 10.5 gpgVery hardFast scale buildup, short tankless heater service intervals. A softener is strongly worth considering.

To convert a ppm reading from a water report, divide by 17.1 to get grains per gallon.

What a proper softener install includes

A softener that is installed badly can cause problems a good unit never would. These are the pieces to expect.

  • 1A bypass valve. It lets you send water around the softener for service or for outdoor use without shutting off the house.
  • 2An air-gapped drain. The brine discharge must empty through an air gap into the drain so waste water cannot be siphoned back into the supply.
  • 3The right loop connection. Many Surprise homes were built with a softener loop in the garage. Using it keeps outdoor hose bibs on unsoftened water.
  • 4A nearby outlet. Most control valves need a standard outlet. An extension cord across the garage floor is not a permanent answer.
  • 5Correct settings. Hardness, household size, and regeneration time are programmed from your test, not left at factory defaults.

Salt-based softener or salt-free conditioner?

Salt-free systems do not remove hardness minerals. They change how the minerals behave so less scale sticks to surfaces. You will still see spots on glass, and a hardness test will read the same afterward.

An ion exchange softener removes the minerals. If your reading lands in the hard or very hard band, and you care about the water heater and a tankless unit most of all, that is the system that actually changes the number.
