Hydro jetting for lines a cable cannot keep open

Hydro jetting is cleaning the inside of a drain or sewer pipe with a stream of water at very high pressure, delivered through a hose and a specialized nozzle. The nozzle's rear jets pull it forward while scouring the pipe walls.

A cable punches a hole through a clog. Jetting removes the coating that caused it, which is why lines that were snaked last month and clogged again often stay open much longer after jetting.

It is not a first step for every drain. Jetting is used when a line is structurally sound but coated with grease, scale, or root hairs, and a camera run confirms that before the jetter comes out.

Cable or jetter? A quick comparison

Both have a place. The choice depends on what is in the pipe.

When to call about hydro jetting

These patterns suggest a coated line rather than a single clog.

  • 1The same drain clogs every few months. Repeat clogs mean something is left behind after snaking.
  • 2Kitchen lines are always slow. Grease coating narrows the pipe a little more each year.
  • 3Roots came back after cutting. Fine root hairs regrow fast unless the pipe wall is cleaned.
  • 4A business kitchen drain backs up. Commercial kitchens build grease far faster than homes.
  • 5A camera showed buildup, not breaks. That is the ideal case for jetting.

When jetting is the wrong tool

High pressure can harm a pipe that is already failing. Cracked, collapsed, or badly corroded sections, and older fragile materials, can be damaged further. That is the reason for the camera first.

If the footage shows a break, the answer is repairing the damaged sewer section, not jetting it. Once the repair is done, jetting can clean the rest of the line.

Jetting also will not fix a sag. A low spot in the line collects waste no matter how clean the pipe walls are.
