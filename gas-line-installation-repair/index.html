Gas line installation and repair for Surprise homes

Gas line work is the installation, extension, or repair of the piping that carries natural gas or propane from the meter or tank to appliances. It covers new runs for ranges and outdoor kitchens as well as fixing leaks in existing lines.

Gas work carries more risk than water work, so it is held to stricter rules. New lines and major changes generally need a city permit and inspection, and every new or repaired line is pressure-tested before the gas is turned on.

Outdoor living is a big driver of gas projects in Surprise. Fire pits, built-in grills, and patio heaters all need properly sized, protected lines, which is where many do-it-yourself setups fall short.

Gas work inside city limits is permitted and inspected by the City of Surprise, while projects in unincorporated Maricopa County go through the county instead. Outdoor kitchens and fire pits are especially common in the newer neighborhoods north of Loop 303.

What to do if you smell gas

This comes before anything else on this page.

  • 1Leave the building. Get everyone out, including pets. Do not stop to open windows.
  • 2Do not create a spark. No light switches, phones, lighters, or garage door openers inside.
  • 3Call from outside. Once you are well away, call 911 or your gas utility's emergency line.
  • 4Leave the meter alone. The utility shuts off and checks the gas. Do not try to turn it back on yourself.
  • 5Call a plumber for the repair. After the utility makes it safe, the leaking line or connection is repaired and tested.

Line sizing and CSST bonding

Adding an appliance changes how much gas the line has to carry. An undersized line starves appliances, so a new tankless heater or outdoor kitchen often means checking the size of the whole run, not just the last few feet.

Many newer homes use yellow-jacketed corrugated stainless steel tubing, called CSST. The International Fuel Gas Code, the companion to the IPC for gas piping, requires it to be bonded to the electrical grounding system, and that bonding should be checked whenever the system is extended.

Insurance and gas line work

Insurance generally does not pay for new gas lines. Damage from a sudden gas incident is a separate matter handled with your insurer.
