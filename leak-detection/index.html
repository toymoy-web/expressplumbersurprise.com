Finding the leak before anything gets opened up

Leak detection is the process of locating a hidden water leak using listening equipment, thermal cameras, pressure tests, and tracer gas, without cutting into walls or floors on a guess. The goal is one small, accurate opening instead of several exploratory ones.

Household leaks are easy to underestimate. According to the EPA's WaterSense program, leaks in an average home can waste nearly 10,000 gallons a year. Many callers do not know what is wrong yet. They only know the bill jumped, the meter keeps turning, or there is a sound of running water with every tap closed. Leak detection starts right there, with the symptom, and works back to the source.

In Surprise, slab foundations, attic plumbing runs in many newer homes, and long yard lines to irrigation and pools give a leak plenty of places to hide. Knowing how your house is plumbed narrows the search quickly.

Signs you need leak detection even if you cannot see water

These are the symptoms people describe most when a leak is hidden.

  • 1The water bill jumped. Usage went up with no change in habits, pool filling, or landscaping.
  • 2The meter keeps moving. With every fixture off, the leak indicator on the meter face still turns.
  • 3You hear running water. A hiss or trickle in a wall or under the floor when nothing is on.
  • 4A floor feels warm. A warm spot on tile often means a hot water line leaking under the slab.
  • 5Stains or bubbling paint. Ceiling stains below attic lines or bubbling baseboard paint point to water behind the surface.

Do this two-minute meter test first

This test tells you whether water is escaping somewhere before anyone comes out.

  • 1Turn everything off. Close every faucet, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no toilet is refilling.
  • 2Pause the irrigation. Set the landscape controller to off and turn off any pool autofill.
  • 3Read the meter. Find the small leak indicator on the meter face, often a triangle or star, and watch it for two minutes.
  • 4Close the house main. If the indicator moves, shut the house main. If it stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house.
  • 5Write it down. Note what you saw. It saves real time when the plumber arrives.

The tools used to find hidden leaks

Different leaks call for different tools, and most jobs use more than one.

MethodHow it worksBest for
Acoustic listeningSensitive ground and wall microphones pick up the sound of water escaping under pressure.Slab leaks and buried supply lines.
Thermal imagingAn infrared camera shows temperature differences from wet or warm areas.Hot water leaks and wet spots behind drywall.
Pressure isolationSections of the system are shut off and watched for pressure drop.Confirming which line is leaking.
Tracer gasA safe gas is pushed into a drained line and detected where it escapes to the surface.Very small leaks and lines under concrete or yards.
Moisture metersProbes measure dampness in drywall, wood, and flooring.Mapping how far water has spread.

Where leaks hide in Surprise homes

Under the slab is the classic spot. Supply lines that run below the concrete can develop pinholes or wear where they rub against the slab, and the only early sign may be a warm floor or a higher bill. Those leaks have their own repair choices, covered on our slab leak detection and repair page.

Overhead is the second spot. Many newer Surprise homes run water lines through the attic, where summer heat is hard on fittings. A leak there shows up as a ceiling stain, sometimes far from the fixture it feeds.

Outside is the third. The service line from the meter to the house, irrigation valves, and pool autofill lines can all leak into the soil. A line that runs from the meter box to the house is a separate job, handled by water line repair and replacement.

Across Maricopa County, a hidden leak usually shows up first on the water bill, whether that bill comes from EPCOR or the City of Surprise. Both place meter boxes near the street, so the meter test works the same from Marley Park to the homes along Grand Avenue.

Leaks, insurance, and paperwork

Many policies pay to access and repair damage from a sudden hidden leak, and some cover the cost of finding it. Slow leaks that ran for months are often excluded, so call your agent with the detection report in hand.

After the leak is found

A leak in a single spot is usually repaired right there. When a home keeps springing leaks in different places, especially older copper, it may be time to talk about repiping instead of patching.
