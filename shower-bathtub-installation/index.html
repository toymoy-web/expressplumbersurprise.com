Shower and bathtub installation done to code

Shower and bathtub installation covers setting a new tub or shower pan, installing the valve and drain, and connecting the supply and waste lines. It includes simple swaps as well as conversions that change the layout of a bathroom.

The valve inside the wall is the most important part. The IPC requires shower valves to be pressure-balancing, thermostatic, or a combination, so a flushed toilet elsewhere in the house cannot send a burst of scalding water.

Older valves often lack that protection, and replacing them is a real safety upgrade. It also fixes the drips and stiff handles that hard water causes over the years.

Remodels here are permitted by the City of Surprise, and hard water from EPCOR or city wells is the main reason new glass and valves scale up. Conversions are especially common in Sun City Grand, a large active adult community in Maricopa County.

What to do before a shower or tub project

Planning ahead keeps the bathroom out of service for less time.

  • 1Pick fixtures early. Valves and trim must match, so choose the brand and style before work starts.
  • 2Plan access needs. Grab bar blocking, a bench, or a handheld shower are easiest to add during the job.
  • 3Check the water heater. A large soaking tub may need more hot water than the current tank delivers.
  • 4Arrange a second bathroom. The bathroom will be out of use for part of the project.
  • 5Ask about permits. Layout changes and some valve work may need a city permit.

Hard water and new showers

Scale shows up fast on glass doors, showerheads, and valve cartridges in hard water. It clogs spray nozzles and stiffens handles within a few years.

If you are investing in a new shower, a hardness test is worth doing. Softer water keeps the new glass clear and the valve working smoothly.
