When repiping beats another repair

Repiping is replacing all or most of a home's water supply lines with new pipe. It is the answer when the pipe itself is the problem, so fixing one leak just moves the next leak somewhere else.

The usual signs are a pattern rather than a single event: several slab leaks, pinholes in copper in different rooms, or original polybutylene pipe that insurers and inspectors flag.

A repipe is a big project, but it is finished in days in most homes. New lines are run through walls and ceilings rather than under the slab, which also removes the risk of future slab leaks in the old lines.

Repipes in Surprise are permitted through the City of Surprise, and many requests come from the older parts of town near Bell Road and Kingswood Parke. Across Maricopa County, homes of that age are reaching the point where lines fail in more than one spot.

PEX or copper?

Both are approved materials under the IPC. Each has trade-offs.

Cross-linked polyethylene

PEX is flexible, so it can be routed through walls and ceilings with fewer fittings and less drywall opened. It resists scale and handles expansion well.

It must be kept out of direct sunlight, which matters for any line in an exposed location.

Good fit when

Most whole-home repipes

Usually faster to install and lower in cost.

Type L or M copper

Copper is durable and long-proven, and some owners prefer it. It costs more in material and labor.

In aggressive water it can develop pinholes over time, so water chemistry is part of the decision.

Good fit when

Owner preference or matching existing lines

Heavier and slower to install than PEX.

What to do to get ready for a repipe

Preparation shortens the job and protects your home.

  • 1Clear access to walls. Move furniture and wall hangings where lines will be run.
  • 2Plan for water outages. Water is off for parts of the job. Ask for the schedule.
  • 3Ask about patching. Confirm whether drywall patching and paint are included.
  • 4Confirm the permit. A whole-home repipe generally needs a city permit and inspection.
  • 5Protect valuables. Cover furniture and electronics in rooms where drywall will be opened.

Insurance and repiping

Insurance rarely pays for repiping, since it is considered an upgrade. Some insurers require replacing polybutylene before writing a policy.
