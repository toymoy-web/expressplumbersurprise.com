Where our Surprise plumbers work

Our service area centers on Surprise and reaches outward along Grand Avenue and Loop 303. It includes suburban neighborhoods on city sewer, active adult communities, older towns with 1950s plumbing, and rural lots on septic and private wells.

Each area has its own patterns. A 1950s Youngtown house fails differently from a 2010s Marley Park home, and a Wittmann property with a well needs different parts than a Sun City West duplex. The town pages below explain what to expect in each.

According to U.S. Census counts, Surprise more than tripled in population between 2000 and 2010, so most of its homes date from the same building boom. Inside Surprise itself, the biggest divide is age. Kingswood Parke and the early Sun City Grand streets date from the 1990s, while Marley Park and the growth north of Loop 303 are much newer. Older homes need valves, supply lines, and water heaters replaced, and newer ones more often need a loose fitting or a pressure regulator set correctly.

Sewer, septic, and the water that comes out of the tap

Inside Surprise, most homes are connected to city sewer, and water comes from either EPCOR or the city, depending on the neighborhood. Knowing which utility serves you matters when you are sizing a softener or looking up your water quality report.

The picture changes as you head west and north. Larger lots in Waddell and around Wittmann are more likely to rely on septic tanks, and some draw from private wells. Those homes need plumbers who are comfortable with pressure tanks, long service lines, and septic backups, not just city fixtures.

If you are not sure what your home is connected to, a quick look at your bills helps. A sewer charge means city sewer. No water bill usually means a well. You can also call (623) 301-9042 and ask.
