Is your pool leaking or just evaporating?

Pool leak detection is finding where water escapes from a pool's shell, plumbing, or equipment. Pool leak repair fixes the leaking line, fitting, or component once it is located.

In Surprise, evaporation is the first thing to rule out. Hot, dry air and strong summer sun pull a lot of water out of an open pool, and an autofill can hide both evaporation and a real leak by quietly topping the pool up.

A simple bucket test separates the two. If the pool drops faster than the water in the bucket, something other than evaporation is at work.

Run the bucket test first

It costs nothing and takes a day.

  • 1Fill a bucket with pool water. Set it on a pool step so it sits in the same sun and temperature.
  • 2Mark both levels. Mark the water level inside the bucket and the pool level on the outside of the bucket.
  • 3Turn off the autofill. Otherwise it will refill the pool and hide the loss.
  • 4Wait 24 hours. Run the pump as usual and do not add water.
  • 5Compare the drops. If the pool dropped noticeably more than the bucket, there is likely a leak.

The autofill blind spot

An autofill is a direct link between drinking water and pool water, so the IPC requires backflow protection on it. It makes pool care easier, but it can also hide a leak for months. The pool always looks full, and the only sign is a water bill that keeps climbing.

If your bill jumped and the pool is the likely cause, shut off the autofill and watch the level for a couple of days. A pool that drops much faster than a bucket of its own water needs a closer look.

The autofill supply line itself can leak underground too. That is plumbing work, and it ties into whole-property leak detection when the source is not obvious.

Pools are a big part of backyard life across Maricopa County, and in Surprise the autofill draws EPCOR or City of Surprise water like every other tap. In Marley Park and the neighborhoods north of Loop 303, autofills are easy to forget about until the bill arrives.

Insurance and pool leaks

Insurance usually does not pay for pool leak repairs. Some policies may cover sudden damage caused by a pool leak, but gradual leaks are generally excluded.
