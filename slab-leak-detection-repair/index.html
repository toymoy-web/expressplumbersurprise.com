A slab leak is not a foundation failure. It is a leaking pipe that happens to run under the foundation.

Slab leak detection and repair for Surprise homes

A slab leak is a leak in a water supply line that runs beneath a home's concrete foundation. Slab leak repair either opens the concrete at the exact spot to fix the pipe or reroutes the water line around the slab so the old buried section is abandoned.

Surprise is almost entirely slab-on-grade construction, so any water line routed under the concrete is a candidate. The IPC requires water piping to be protected where it passes through or under concrete, because contact and movement wear pipe over time. Hot water lines leak more often than cold ones, since they expand and contract with every use and can rub against the concrete or gravel around them.

Many people calling about a slab leak do not use that term. They mention a warm floor, water coming up at the garage slab edge, or a water bill that jumped. That is where the search starts.

Slab foundations are the norm across Maricopa County, and Surprise is no exception from Kingswood Parke to Marley Park. Whether your water comes from EPCOR or the City of Surprise, a slab leak still runs through your meter and shows on the bill.

Signs of a slab leak you might notice first

These are the clues people describe, often before they know the cause.

  • 1A warm or hot spot on the floor. Tile or wood that feels warm underfoot often sits above a leaking hot water line.
  • 2The sound of running water. A hiss or trickle with every tap closed points to a pressurized leak.
  • 3Water at the slab edge. Moisture seeping at the edge of the garage or along a baseboard can be coming from under the slab.
  • 4A higher bill with no new use. Water lost under the slab still runs through the meter.
  • 5Cracks or damp flooring. New cracks in tile or damp carpet with no plumbing above it are worth checking.

How a slab leak is pinpointed

Opening concrete on a guess is expensive. These methods find the spot first.

Method, in orderWhat it tells you
Meter and pressure testConfirms a leak exists and whether it is on the hot or cold side.
Line isolationNarrows the leak to a specific branch.
Acoustic listeningPinpoints the sound of water escaping through the slab.
Thermal imagingShows the heat pattern of a hot water leak on the floor surface.
Marking the spotGives the exact location and depth for the repair choice.

Spot repair or reroute?

Both are legitimate fixes. Which one fits depends on where the leak is and the age of the plumbing.

Open the slab at the leak

The concrete is cut and removed at the marked spot, the pipe is repaired, and the slab is patched. Flooring over the spot has to come up and be replaced.

This makes sense when the leak is in an accessible area and the rest of the piping is in good condition.

Good fit when

One leak, sound pipe elsewhere

Keeps the original layout but disturbs the floor at that spot.

Bypass the buried line

A new line is run through walls or the attic to replace the leaking under-slab section, which is abandoned in place. The floor is not opened.

This is often the better choice when the leak is under finished flooring or the buried lines are aging.

Good fit when

Leak under finished floors

Avoids breaking the slab and removes the risk of the next leak in the same line.

Replace the whole system

When a home has had more than one slab leak, the pattern suggests the rest of the buried piping is failing too.

A whole-home repipe moves the water lines out of the slab entirely.

Good fit when

Repeated slab leaks

Our repiping page covers whole-home replacement.

Shut off the hot water if you suspect a slab leak

If the warm spot suggests a hot water line, close the cold water valve on top of the water heater. That stops the flow to the leaking line and keeps the water heater from running constantly to replace hot water that is escaping.

Keep the gas or power to the heater off while its supply is closed. Then call, and keep an eye on the meter.

Insurance and slab leaks

Many policies cover the cost to access a slab leak and repair resulting water damage, but not the pipe repair itself. Coverage varies widely, so call your insurer with the detection report.
