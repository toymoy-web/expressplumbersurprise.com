Water heater repair around the clock when the hot water quits

Water heater repair means finding and replacing the specific part that stopped the unit from heating or started it leaking, without replacing the whole heater. On a gas tank that is often the thermocouple or gas control valve. On an electric tank it is usually a heating element or thermostat.

Our emergency water heater repair line runs 24/7 because no hot water on a winter morning, or a relief valve spraying in the garage, is not something to wait out. The plumber confirms the cause, gives the price, and makes the repair.

Repair only makes sense when the tank itself is sound. When water comes from the tank body, the tank is done, and the honest answer is replacement, not a part.

What to do first when a water heater acts up

A water heater stores scalding water under pressure and, on gas units, burns fuel. Take these steps before anything else.

  • 1Smell gas? Get out. Leave the house without flipping any switches. Once you are clear of the building, phone the gas utility or 911.
  • 2Relief valve discharging? Keep clear of the pipe and shut off the gas or breaker to the heater. The IPC requires this temperature and pressure relief valve on every storage heater, and constant discharge means it is doing its job against excess heat or pressure.
  • 3Tank leaking? Close the cold water valve above the heater and switch off its gas or power.
  • 4Protect the floor. Move stored items away from the tank and put towels or a pan where water is heading.
  • 5Check the breaker or pilot. A tripped breaker or a pilot blown out by a gust can be the entire problem. If it trips or goes out again, call.

How hard water and garage heat wear out heaters here

Scale is the quiet enemy of Surprise water heaters. Minerals settle to the bottom of the tank as a layer that the burner or element has to heat through. That is what causes popping sounds, longer heating times, and overheated tank bottoms.

Flushing a few gallons from the drain valve once or twice a year slows the buildup. On an older tank that has never been flushed, though, a heavy flush can loosen enough sediment to clog the drain valve, so it is worth having someone look first.

Heat is the other factor. Garage air in a Surprise summer often runs well above the temperature inside the house, which adds stress on thermostats, valves, and plastic parts. A unit that fails in August often had a part that was already weakened.

Scale is heavier in some parts of Maricopa County than others, and in Surprise it depends on which of the two suppliers, EPCOR or the City of Surprise, serves your home. Repair calls from the older streets off Bell Road often turn up tanks with years of sediment on the bottom.

Insurance and water heater failures

Insurance generally treats a worn-out part as maintenance and will not pay for the repair. A sudden tank failure that floods the garage may be covered for the water damage, so take photos first.

Water heater repair questions from Surprise

Yes, the emergency line is answered 24/7. If the tank is leaking or the relief valve is discharging, that is handled as urgent.

The gas control valve may not be opening the main burner, or the thermostat is set too low. On older units a failing thermocouple can also cause intermittent shutdowns.

Sediment has built up on the bottom of the tank. Water trapped under it boils and pops. Flushing helps if caught early.

A leak from a fitting is a nuisance. A leak from the tank body means the tank has failed and can release more water without warning. Shut off the cold supply and power or gas, then call.

The smell comes from a reaction between sulfate-reducing bacteria and the anode rod. It is usually a nuisance rather than a hazard, and swapping the anode rod type often fixes it.

If the tank is under about 8 years old and not leaking, a repair usually makes sense. If it is leaking from the body or the repair costs near half a new unit, replacement is the better value.
