Toilet repair that finds the worn part first

Toilet repair means restoring a toilet to a proper flush and a dry base by replacing whatever part has failed. The usual suspects are the flapper, the fill valve, the supply line, and the wax ring that seals the toilet to the floor.

A running toilet is more than an annoyance. A flapper that leaks into the bowl can waste a surprising amount of water without a sound, and it shows up on your water bill before you notice anything else.

Most Surprise homes were built after 1994, when federal rules limited new toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush. That means the original toilets here are low-flow models, and the parts inside them are now 20 to 30 years old in many neighborhoods.

What to do when a toilet overflows

Speed matters more than anything here.

  • 1Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down. That stops more water from entering the bowl right away.
  • 2Turn the shutoff behind the toilet clockwise. This stops the tank from refilling.
  • 3Do not flush again. A second flush on a clogged toilet sends more water onto the floor.
  • 4Check other drains. If a tub or shower also backs up, the problem is likely the main sewer line, not the toilet.
  • 5Dry the floor quickly. Water that sits around the base can soak into the subfloor and cabinets.

Hard water and toilet parts

Mineral scale is a quiet cause of toilet trouble in Surprise. Scale collects on the flapper seat and inside the fill valve, so the seal stops closing fully and the valve sticks.

It also clogs the small rim holes under the bowl edge that direct water during the flush. A toilet that flushes weakly but is not clogged often has scaled jets rather than a blockage.

If you are replacing toilet parts every couple of years, it is worth testing your water. Scale that shortens a fill valve's life is also working on your water heater and faucets.

Across Maricopa County, toilets in 1990s and 2000s homes were installed to the 1.6 gallon limit, and hard water from EPCOR or the City of Surprise slowly scales their fill valves. In Sun City Grand, where homes date from 1996 onward, those parts are now due.

Repair or replace the toilet?

A few questions settle it.

Replace the failing part

A sound porcelain bowl and tank with one worn part almost always gets repaired. Flappers, fill valves, and wax rings are inexpensive.

This is the right call for most toilets in homes built in the last 30 years.

Choose this if

The porcelain is intact

Parts are standard and widely available.

Install a new toilet

A cracked bowl or tank, a toilet that clogs constantly, or one where parts keep failing is a candidate for replacement.

A new WaterSense model uses 1.28 gallons per flush or less, about 20 percent less than the 1.6 gallon standard.

Cracks or constant clogs

Fix what the toilet sits on

A broken flange is why some toilets keep leaking at the base even after a new wax ring. The flange anchors the toilet to the drain.

Repair rings and replacement flanges restore a solid mount.

The base leaks again and again

Insurance and toilet leaks

A slow leak at the wax ring that rots the subfloor is usually treated as maintenance and not covered. A sudden supply line burst that floods the bathroom may be covered for the water damage.
